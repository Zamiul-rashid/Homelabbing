## What I Ended Up With
A lightning-fast, privacy-first smart home hub running directly on my home server's network adapter (`port 8123`). Home Assistant automatically scanned my Wi-Fi network to discover and pair with my Apple HomeKit, Sonos, Google Cast, Philips Hue, and Zigbee devices. This allowed me to control everything from one single app even when my internet connection dropped.

## Why I Chose Home Assistant

Commercial smart home gadgets routinely relied on cloud servers: when I pressed a switch on my wall, the signal traveled to a data center across the country to turn on the bulb above my head. I moved to Home Assistant so it executed my automations 100% locally with zero latency and complete privacy.

## How I Set It Up

### 1. Launching the Container
I navigated to the `home-automation` stack directory and launched the compose stack:
```bash
cd stacks/home-automation
docker compose up -d
```

### 2. First-Run Wizard
I opened `http://YOUR_SERVER_IP:8123` in my web browser. Home Assistant took about 1–2 minutes on first boot to prepare its internal database and required dependencies. Once it was ready, I followed the wizard to set my name, username, and home location (used for sunrise/sunset triggers).

### 3. Pairing My First Devices
1. Once logged in, I went to **Settings** → **Devices & Services**.
2. Home Assistant automatically displayed discovered devices on my network right under the **Discovered** banner.
3. I clicked **Configure** on devices like my Apple TV, Sonos, or Philips Hue to pair them instantly without needing cloud accounts.
