## What I Ended Up With
My simpler apps stay up to date on their own. Watchtower checks for new images, pulls them, and recreates each container with the same options it had before. A notification tells me what changed.

## Why I Chose Watchtower

Keeping many containers current by hand means pulling and recreating each one. For small, stable apps that is wasted time, and Watchtower does it with one container.

**A maintenance note:** the original `containrrr/watchtower` repository was archived in December 2025 and is no longer maintained. It still works, but community forks such as `nicholas-fedor/watchtower` carry on development. Check the state of whichever you choose before you rely on it.

## How I Set It Up

### 1. Opt In, Not Out
I set Watchtower to update only containers with its enable label, and added that label to small apps that rarely break.

### 2. Leaving Some Alone
Databases and apps that often ship breaking changes, Immich being a common example, are better left unlabelled and updated by hand after reading the release notes.

### 3. Notifications
I configured a notification so every update shows up as a message, which makes it easy to connect a broken app to a recent update.
